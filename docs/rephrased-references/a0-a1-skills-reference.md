# Ella · A0→A1 Skill Labels & Icons

**Design Reference · NavGurukul · July 2026**

Revised student-facing labels for all 20 skills across the 5 units of the A0→A1 path.
Original "I can" statements included for review and handoff.

**Track key:** 🌸 Vocabulary · 🔵 Grammar · 🟣 Fluency

> **Context:** This level was added for the pilot cohort — students who are literate in their regional language but whose spoken English is below A1. The transition covers "silence → naming → describing yourself." Grammar stays entirely in the present tense. The fluency milestone: a student attempts to speak and sustains 2–3 turns.

> **Structural note:** Step 5 has no Vocabulary skill (2 Grammar + 2 Fluency). This is the only step across the entire framework (A0→C1) that has zero skills in one of the three tracks. Confirm with Prachi whether this is intentional or whether a missing skill needs adding.

> **Level naming (open):** A1 is "First Words." The student-facing name for A0 has not been decided. A name that implies "before first words" risks being deficit-framed. Needs discussion with Prachi before this level ships.

## Open Items for Review

- **Prachi — Step 5 has no Vocabulary skill.** Every other step in the entire framework (A0→C1) includes at least one Vocabulary skill. This is either a deliberate design choice (Step 5 is integration-focused) or an omission. If intentional, the garden needs to handle a unit with no pink/rose-track plant. Confirm before Figma build.
- **Prachi — Level name for A0.** "First Words" (A1) implies a starting point. A level named *below* that risks telling students they haven't started yet. Consider names that frame A0 as a launchpad: "Getting Ready," "Before the Garden," "Starting Out," or a Hindi/English hybrid. This is a high-stakes naming decision — it sets the emotional frame for the students with the lowest confidence.
- **Prachi — Skill 20 vs A1→A2 Skill 19.** "Keep trying even with just a few words" (A0) and "Keep speaking even when you're unsure" (A1→A2) are near-identical courage/mindset skills. This is likely intentional reinforcement across levels, but confirm — and if so, should these always appear as the final skill in every transition?
- **Prachi — Skill 4 ("Point at something and name it").** The "I can" statement says "when someone points at them" — this implies a teacher-led or in-person prompt. In the app, Zoe can't point at things. How does this skill get evidenced in a voice conversation? The evaluation prompt may need a specific strategy for this.
- **Sael — Icon conflicts across levels.** Some icons overlap with A1→A2 assignments (💬 used in both A0 Unit 3 and A1→A2 Unit 5; 💪 vs 🌟 are parallel courage icons). If icons are unique per-level this is fine, but if the icon library is global, deduplication is needed.
- **Sael — A0 icons should be extremely literal.** At this level students may not parse abstract iconography at all. The emoji placeholders here lean concrete (🐾 🎒 📍 ❤️), but Sael should validate that the illustrated versions are equally literal. A concept icon that requires English comprehension to decode defeats the purpose.

## Labelling principle at A0

At every other level, labels either use "examples as label" (the actual English words *are* the concept) or describe a communicative action. At A0, both strategies are used but skewed heavily toward examples-as-label — because the examples are the only English the student knows. Labels like "Say I am happy or I am not tired" put real English sentences on the card. The student can read the label, attempt it, and recognise it when Zoe says it back. The label is simultaneously a title, an example, and a prompt.
